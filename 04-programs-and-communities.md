# 04 — Programs, Hackathons & Communities

> ⚠️ Deadlines, stipends and eligibility change every year. Always confirm on the official website. Most programs open applications months before they start — set calendar reminders.

## Mentored open-source programs

| Program | What it is | Typical timing | Notes |
|---------|-----------|----------------|-------|
| **Google Summer of Code (GSoC)** | Paid, mentored open-source project with a partner org over the summer | Orgs announced ~Feb, proposals ~Mar–Apr | Start contributing to your target org **before** proposals open |
| **Outreachy** | Paid remote internships in open source for people underrepresented in tech | Two cohorts per year (May & Dec starts) | Has an initial application + contribution period |
| **LFX Mentorship (Linux Foundation)** | Mentored projects in Linux Foundation/CNCF projects (Kubernetes, etc.) | Several terms per year | Great for cloud/systems interests |
| **MLH Fellowship** | Remote program working on open-source or production projects in pods | Multiple batches per year | Strong community aspect |
| **Season of KDE / GNOME internships** | Mentored contributions to Linux desktop projects | Varies | Friendly to beginners |
| **Summer of Bitcoin** | Paid open-source internships in Bitcoin projects | Summer | Niche but well-structured |
| **Hacktoberfest** | October event encouraging quality contributions | October | Good motivation for first PRs — focus on quality |
| **Google Season of Docs** | Technical writing for open-source projects | Varies | Great if you like documentation |

### How to win GSoC / Outreachy
1. Choose an org **3–4 months early**.
2. Join their chat (Zulip/Discord/Slack/mailing list), introduce yourself briefly.
3. Get 2–5 PRs merged in that org.
4. Discuss your proposal idea with mentors before writing it.
5. Write a proposal with: problem, detailed plan, week-by-week timeline, deliverables, and why you (link merged PRs).

## Hackathons & competitions

- **MLH (Major League Hacking)** — lists hundreds of student hackathons, many online
- **Devpost** — hackathon listings and project showcase
- **Kaggle** — ML competitions, datasets, notebooks; great for building ML skills and a public profile
- **AIcrowd, DrivenData, Zindi** — ML challenges, often with social-impact themes
- **Google Solution Challenge**, **Microsoft Imagine Cup** — student competitions for apps solving real problems
- **ICPC / Codeforces / LeetCode contests** — competitive programming (good for problem solving and interviews)
- **University and company hackathons** — check your campus clubs

**Hackathon tip:** after the event, spend one more weekend polishing the project and writing it up. Most teams don't — that's how yours stands out.

## Communities worth joining

**General dev**
- Your university's ACM / IEEE / Google Developer Student Club chapter
- dev.to and Hashnode (blogging communities)
- The official Discord/Zulip of tools you contribute to

**AI / ML**
- Hugging Face Discord & community events
- EleutherAI, Cohere Labs community, ML Collective, LAION (see [02-research.md](02-research.md))
- Papers We Love / local ML reading groups
- Kaggle forums

**Diversity-focused**
- Women Who Code-style local chapters, Rewriting the Code, AnitaB.org / Grace Hopper Celebration
- Outreachy community
- Black in AI, LatinX in AI, Queer in AI, Women in Machine Learning (WiML) — these also run workshops at major ML conferences that welcome undergrads

## Conferences (as a student)

- Many conferences offer **student volunteer** spots (free registration) and **travel grants** — apply!
- Look for **student research workshops / undergrad poster sessions**.
- Local meetups (PyData, GDG, ML meetups) are free and great for networking.
- PyCon and similar language conferences often have **sprints** — days where you contribute to open-source projects with maintainers in the room.

## Fellowships & scholarships to keep an eye on

- University-specific research scholarships and summer funds
- Company scholarships for students in computing (search "[company] student scholarship computer science")
- Open-source grant programs (some foundations fund small projects)
